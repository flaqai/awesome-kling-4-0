# Short Drama & Viral Social Prompts

[← Catalog](README.md) · [Prompting guide](../docs/PROMPT-GUIDE.md) · [Multilingual audio](../docs/MULTILINGUAL-AUDIO.md)

Four original Kling recipes for episodic cliffhangers, dialogue comedy, mockumentary spectacle and surreal social loops. They are designed around fast comprehension rather than copyrighted characters, generated captions or imitation of a named filmmaker.

## 1. The Metro Card That Came Back

**Use case:** vertical episodic-drama cliffhanger · **Format:** 15s, 9:16 · **Best mode:** two character references + location reference

**Why it works:** The episode establishes one prop, one emotional reversal and one unanswered final image without forcing a complete story into 15 seconds.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 9:16, contemporary vertical micro-drama, restrained realism, four fast but readable shots, episode-one cliffhanger.

[CONTINUITY ANCHORS]
Mina: adult woman, short black bob, camel coat, canvas tote on left shoulder. Theo: adult station attendant, shaved head, navy uniform with no badge. Prop: one scratched green transit card with a small crescent-shaped corner chip; no readable printing.

[GEOGRAPHY]
Quiet underground station after the evening rush. Mina stands outside the closed service window, Theo inside. The card begins in Mina's right hand. Camera never crosses the service-window axis.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–4s — Tight handheld medium. Mina slides the green card through the tray and says in Mandarin: “这张卡不是我的。” Theo looks down, then freezes before touching it.
4–8s — Insert from Mina's side. Theo turns the card over with one gloved fingertip. The crescent chip is clearly visible. He asks quietly in English: “Where did you find this?”
8–12s — Close on Mina. Her defensive expression softens; she answers: “Inside my locked apartment.” A train passes behind camera, briefly sweeping light across her face without hiding the cut.
12–15s — Close behind Theo's shoulder. He opens a shallow drawer containing one second, identical chipped green card in a clear evidence sleeve. He does not remove it. Mina sees it and steps back once. Cut to black before either speaks.

[AUDIO]
Station ventilation, distant train, tray scrape and drawer rail. Only Mina speaks the Mandarin line and final English line; only Theo asks the question. One low tonal hit when the drawer opens; no subtitles.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Two adults and exactly two matching cards by the final shot. Preserve chip position, wardrobe, tote shoulder, window axis and eyelines. No police insignia, threat, weapon, supernatural glow, generated text, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** card → key / photograph / sealed letter; the duplicate must be established visually before the cut to black.

**Failure check:** Verify card count, chip placement, speaker ownership and that the drawer reveal remains visible for at least one second.

## 2. The Last Clean Mug

**Use case:** workplace dialogue comedy · **Format:** 12s, 9:16 · **Best mode:** three character elements + custom multi-shot

**Why it works:** One disputed prop, fixed seating and short lines create a clear setup–escalation–payoff rhythm.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 9:16, dry workplace comedy, ordinary break-room lighting, four shots with precise reaction timing.

[CHARACTERS AND PROP]
Omar: adult man in green knit vest, seated frame left. Leena: adult woman in blue overshirt, standing frame right. Jo: adult nonbinary colleague in gray hoodie, initially soft background center. One plain yellow ceramic mug sits beside the sink. No other mug is visible.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–3s — Locked two-shot. Omar and Leena reach for the yellow mug at the same moment but stop before contact. Omar says in English: “I washed it.”
3–6s — Reverse close on Leena, hand still hovering. She answers: “You watched me wash it.” Omar lowers his hand, caught.
6–9s — Rack focus from their frozen hands to Jo in the background, calmly drinking from a small metal camping cup brought from home. Jo glances at them but says nothing.
9–12s — Return to the two-shot. Omar and Leena look at the yellow mug, then simultaneously step away. The mug handle slowly rotates toward camera because the drying mat settles, producing an outsized ceramic squeak. Both turn back suspiciously. Hard cut.

[AUDIO]
Refrigerator hum, two exact English lines, fabric movement, one sip and the final ceramic squeak. No laugh track, music or subtitles.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Three adults, one yellow mug and one metal cup. No simultaneous speech, hand collision, mug teleport, animated face on mug or supernatural motion beyond the tiny mat-driven rotation. Preserve positions, wardrobe and prop colors. No text, brand or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** mug → stapler / chair / last pastry; keep the final motion physically explainable but comically suspicious.

**Failure check:** Confirm only the named speaker's lips move and the yellow mug never changes hands or duplicates.

## 3. Laundry Day Above the Harbor

**Use case:** giant-object mockumentary hook · **Format:** 12s, 9:16 · **Best mode:** city start image + single-take phone footage

**Why it works:** The spectacle is one harmless household object with measurable scale, consistent wind and grounded witness reactions.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 9:16, convincing phone-shot mockumentary, one continuous take from a public harbor promenade, playful surrealism.

[SPECTACLE LOCK]
One enormous empty wicker laundry basket, approximately 25m wide, floating 40m above the water. Natural tan weave, oval opening facing slightly downward, two handles. It never contacts buildings, boats or people.

[CAMERA AND ACTION]
0–3s — Phone camera points at calm harbor and three adult pedestrians. A broad basket-shaped shadow passes slowly across the pavement from left to right. Witnesses stop and look upward.
3–7s — Camera operator tilts up late and briefly loses focus, revealing the floating basket drifting left-to-right at walking speed. A single red sock hangs over its rim and flutters in the same wind direction.
7–10s — Camera digitally zooms only 1.3×. One handle creaks; the sock slips, falls five meters, then catches safely on the lower weave. Witness off-camera says in English: “Whose laundry day is this?”
10–12s — Camera tilts back to the promenade. One elderly adult calmly raises a clothespin as if volunteering. Hold on the deadpan gesture.

[AUDIO]
Harbor wind, gulls, phone-mic handling, distant wicker creak, one off-camera line and one small clothespin click. No cinematic score or crowd scream.

[CONSTRAINTS]
One basket and one sock. Coherent shadow, scale, perspective, wind and occlusion. Adults stay safely on promenade. No branded skyline, destruction, panic, falling debris, giant animal, generated caption, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** basket → picnic blanket / paper lantern / empty watering can; preserve a benign scale joke and one deadpan witness payoff.

**Failure check:** Track basket, sock and shadow direction; reject any shot where scale jumps or the object threatens people.

## 4. The Shadow Takes the Early Train

**Use case:** surreal commuter loop · **Format:** 8s, 1:1 · **Best mode:** matched start/end frame

**Why it works:** A single impossible rule is isolated to the shadow while the person, lighting and camera remain ordinary.

```text
[OUTPUT]
8-second seamless 1:1 social loop, locked camera, realistic morning station entrance with one controlled surreal event.

[LOCKS]
Adult commuter Nia wears a pale-gray coat and carries one red tote in right hand. Low sun from frame left casts her shadow diagonally toward frame right. One blank train-entrance wall, no readable signs.

[LOOP ACTION]
0–2s — Nia stands still checking the closed face of a plain wristwatch. Her shadow matches exactly.
2–4s — The shadow alone looks toward the entrance, taps its foot twice and begins walking toward frame right while Nia remains still. Shadow keeps Nia's silhouette and tote shape.
4–6s — Nia notices the departing shadow, sighs, and takes two quick steps after it. At the doorway edge, her foot contacts the shadow's trailing foot and they merge cleanly under natural overlap.
6–8s — Nia steps backward along the same path to the exact opening pose; shadow, tote, coat hem and wrist return to their first-frame positions. Ambient light and background pedestrians also reset without a visible jump.

[AUDIO]
Seamless station ambience, two shadow foot taps represented by dry shoe sounds, Nia's small sigh and two steps. No speech or music.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Only the shadow behaves independently. One person, one tote, one continuous shadow and fixed sun direction. No detached body parts, second person, face in shadow, teleport outside overlap, generated text, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** shadow → reflection in a dark window / footprint trail / coat silhouette; give only one visual layer independent agency.

**Failure check:** Play three loops and verify first/last pose, shadow angle, pedestrian positions and audio bed align.
