# People, Pets, Learning & Culture Prompts

[← Catalog](README.md) · [Prompting guide](../docs/PROMPT-GUIDE.md) · [Multilingual audio](../docs/MULTILINGUAL-AUDIO.md)

Four original Kling recipes for direct-to-camera explanation, service avatars, animal profiles and child-safe science. Obtain consent for every identifiable person and voice; adult supervision and factual review remain essential.

## 1. The Forty-Second Architecture Lesson

**Use case:** talking-head course opener · **Format:** 15s, 16:9 · **Best mode:** presenter reference + single take

**Why it works:** Short phrasing, one physical prop and deliberate pauses keep eye contact and mouth motion manageable.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 16:9, approachable online-course opener, one continuous medium shot, realistic studio finish.

[PRESENTER LOCK]
Dr. Salma, adult architect with shoulder-length dark curls, round amber glasses and navy linen jacket. She stands behind a light wood table. One blank white card model with a doorway cutout sits center. Preserve identity, wardrobe and model geometry.

[CAMERA]
Locked eye-level 50mm composition, waist-up. Very slow 3% push-in begins after 5s. Salma looks into lens except during the model demonstration.

[ACTION AND SPEECH]
0–4s — Salma says in English: “A room is not only its walls.” Brief breath; hands rest beside the model.
4–10s — She turns the model 30 degrees clockwise with two fingertips and says: “Watch what happens when the entrance changes.” Her gaze follows the doorway, then returns to lens.
10–15s — She moves one small warm desk light from left to right of the model; the shadow visibly changes. She says in Arabic: “الضوء يرسم الطريق.” End with hands still and a small inviting smile.

[AUDIO]
Close natural voice, card friction and one light-base contact. Quiet treated room, no music or subtitles.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Exact lines and language assignment. One speaking mouth, natural fingers, no model redesign, floating prop or exaggerated presenter gestures. No generated diagrams, text, credentials, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** architecture → cooking technique / photography / language lesson; pair one sentence with one observable prop change.

**Failure check:** Verify Arabic pronunciation with a fluent reviewer and check that each hand action happens after its verbal cue.

## 2. Multilingual Library Helper

**Use case:** consented digital-human service explainer · **Format:** 12s, 9:16 · **Best mode:** approved avatar reference + custom background

**Why it works:** The avatar handles only a narrow navigation task and directs users to real staff rather than inventing policy.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 9:16, friendly public-library welcome clip, clean digital-human presentation, two language turns.

[AVATAR AND SET]
Approved fictional adult avatar Ivo: short auburn hair, forest-green cardigan, cream shirt, small silver circle pin with no mark. Waist-up behind a plain information desk. Background shelves softly focused; blank sign area frame right for real post-production text.

[PERFORMANCE]
0–5s — Ivo faces lens, open relaxed posture, and says in Spanish: “Hola. Para devolver libros, siga las luces azules.” At “luces azules,” right hand makes one small open-palm gesture toward frame right.
5–9s — Half-second pause. Ivo switches to English: “For account questions, a staff member can help at this desk.” Both hands return visibly to the desk.
9–12s — Ivo nods once and looks briefly toward the blank sign area, then back to lens. Hold still for the final second.

[AUDIO]
Clear neutral voice, accurate language switch, low library ambience and distant page turn. No music, announcement or subtitles.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Fictional authorized avatar and voice only. Exact lines; no policy, opening-hours or accessibility claim beyond the script. No lip drift, language mixing, extra fingers, generated signage, books with readable titles, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** library → museum / clinic reception / campus desk; replace with verified local instructions before publishing.

**Failure check:** A bilingual reviewer checks wording and lip sync; staff verifies the physical direction and service promise.

## 3. Pippa's Adoption Portrait

**Use case:** animal shelter profile · **Format:** 12s, 9:16 · **Best mode:** pet + handler + room references

**Why it works:** Observable behavior and calm handling replace invented personality or health claims.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 9:16, warm documentary pet profile, three gentle shots, no generated captions.

[LOCKS]
Pippa: one small adult mixed-breed dog, cream wiry coat, tan left ear, brown eyes and teal harness. Handler Jun: adult, gray trousers and yellow sweater; face remains out of frame. Quiet shelter meeting room with blue rug and low wooden bench.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–4s — Dog-eye-level medium shot. Pippa sits on the blue rug, looks toward an off-camera soft squeak, tilts head once and keeps all four paws grounded.
4–8s — Close side shot. Jun offers a closed relaxed hand from below. Pippa leans forward to sniff, then looks up; Jun does not grab or restrain her.
8–12s — Wider locked shot. Jun rolls one soft fabric ball 60cm across the rug. Pippa follows at a walk, touches it with nose and returns gaze toward Jun. End with clean upper-frame space for verified shelter details in post.

[AUDIO]
Quiet room, light collar tag, paws on rug and one soft toy roll. No bark requirement, sentimental narration or music swell.

[CONSTRAINTS]
One dog, one handler and one ball. Preserve coat pattern, ear color, harness and room. Calm voluntary behavior; no forced contact, cage, child, health claim, breed certainty, generated adoption status, text, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** dog → cat / rabbit / senior pet; describe only behavior actually reviewed by the shelter.

**Failure check:** Check anatomy, coat markings, harness fit and that the final copy is added from verified shelter records.

## 4. Pepper and Soap Science Table

**Use case:** child-safe classroom explainer · **Format:** 15s, 16:9 · **Best mode:** educator + tabletop reference

**Why it works:** An adult controls every material, the camera shows the result clearly and the narration avoids overstating the simple demonstration.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 16:9, bright classroom science demonstration, one overhead insert within a locked medium-wide scene.

[PEOPLE AND MATERIALS]
Adult educator Hana wears blue apron and clear splash glasses. Two school-age children sit opposite her, hands folded until invited. On table: one shallow white plate of water, small dish of ground black pepper, one cotton swab, one pump of liquid soap. No food is consumed.

[TIMED ACTION]
0–5s — Medium-wide. Hana sprinkles a small even layer of pepper onto the water and says in English: “The pepper helps us see movement on the surface.” Children watch; they do not touch materials.
5–10s — Overhead close-up. Hana presses the soap pump once onto the cotton swab, then touches the swab tip to the water center. Pepper moves outward from the contact point; plate stays still.
10–15s — Return wide. Hana removes the swab, places it in a small waste cup and says: “This is a model, not a test of how clean something is.” Children give one shared surprised reaction; Hana smiles.

[AUDIO]
Natural classroom room tone, pepper tap, pump click and restrained child reaction. No music or voice-over.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Adult handles all materials. Preserve two children, one educator and object count. No ingestion, splashing, skin contact, medical claim, germ animation, unsafe chemical, generated text, school logo or watermark. Factual review required.
```

**Swap ideas:** surface tension → shadow length / paper bridge / color mixing; keep risk low and distinguish model from proof.

**Failure check:** Confirm adult-only handling, correct sequence and scientifically careful wording before classroom use.
