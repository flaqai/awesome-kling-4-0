# Genre Action & Sound-Sync Prompts

[← Catalog](README.md) · [Prompting guide](../docs/PROMPT-GUIDE.md) · [Multilingual audio](../docs/MULTILINGUAL-AUDIO.md)

Four original Kling recipes for closed-course racing, mechanical animation, sound-led craft montage and orbital operations. They favor readable physics, controlled environments and owned designs over references to famous films, composers or franchises.

## 1. Wet-Track Precision Lap

**Use case:** closed-course motorsport sequence · **Format:** 15s, 21:9 · **Best mode:** car + driver references, custom multi-shot

**Why it works:** A controlled test track, one vehicle, fixed corner geography and speed-dependent spray create intensity without dangerous public-road behavior.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 21:9, realistic closed-course driver-training film at dawn, four shots, high energy without collisions.

[VEHICLE AND DRIVER]
One compact cobalt-blue track car with white roof, black seven-spoke wheels, blank number panel and no logo. Adult professional driver wears a white certified helmet, blue suit and secured six-point harness. Preserve vehicle panels, wheel design and driver throughout.

[TRACK GEOGRAPHY]
Private wet handling circuit, right-hand hairpin marked by six orange cones and a white apex line. Empty runoff, barriers and marshals safely behind fence. Car travels left-to-right before turning away from camera.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–4s — Interior footwell insert. Right foot releases brake progressively and applies throttle; left foot stays on dead pedal. Water beads move backward on side window. Engine note rises smoothly.
4–8s — Low exterior side tracking. Car approaches left-to-right at controlled speed; tire spray trails behind wheels, never ahead. Camera remains outside barriers.
8–12s — High fixed hairpin view. Driver brakes before cone one, turns once toward the apex and follows the white line without sliding or hitting cones. Brake lights illuminate during deceleration only.
12–15s — Rear three-quarter from safe long lens. Car straightens, accelerates away and spray narrows as it reaches drier pavement. End with all six cones standing.

[AUDIO]
Authentic single engine layer matched across cuts, wet tire hiss, two gear changes and no music until a restrained pulse enters at 12s. No dialogue.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Private course, one car, trained adult, correct harness and intact safety zone. No public traffic, passenger, collision, drift, jump, hydroplane, cone strike, speed claim, dashboard text, badge or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** car → kart / racing bicycle / wheelchair athletics training; redesign geography and safety constraints for the approved sport.

**Failure check:** Track wheel rotation, spray direction, cone count, brake-light timing and corner direction through every cut.

## 2. Clockwork Heron Launch

**Use case:** original mechanical-animation sequence · **Format:** 12s, 16:9 · **Best mode:** character design + mechanism references

**Why it works:** The transformation has a finite inventory and sequential joints, keeping the original animated machine recognizable during fast action.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 16:9, original stylized 3D mechanical animation, brushed brass and indigo enamel, three shots.

[DESIGN LOCK]
One clockwork heron machine: oval brass body, two long folded legs, narrow indigo beak, two segmented wings with exactly five primary plates each, small round amber lens eyes. No pilot, weapon, logo or resemblance to an existing character.

[WORLD]
Quiet reed workshop built on a wooden platform above shallow water at sunrise. One circular launch rail points toward open marsh. Warm light frame right, cool mist low over water.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–4s — Three macro mechanical inserts: one spring winds clockwise, two leg locks release, then ten wing plates unfold in paired sequence from body outward. Cut only on gear contact; each action visibly causes the next.
4–8s — Medium side view. Heron takes exactly three measured steps onto the launch rail. Legs bear weight and flex at the knee joints; head counterbalances forward. Camera tracks at matching speed.
8–12s — Wide. Wings complete one strong downstroke; machine lifts one meter, clears the platform and glides toward marsh. Water ripples from air pressure after the downstroke. Camera cranes slightly but does not chase.

[AUDIO]
Gear ticks, spring tension, three wooden foot contacts, layered brass wing movement and one deep air push. Original two-note woodwind call after takeoff; no dialogue.

[CONSTRAINTS]
One machine, two legs, two wings, five plates per wing. Mechanically connected motion, correct ground contact and delayed water response. No extra parts, organic feathers, fire, battle, copied franchise design, text or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** heron → beetle / seed pod / workshop courier; enumerate all transforming pieces and their causal order.

**Failure check:** Count wing plates before and after unfolding and verify every joint carries weight or aerodynamic force plausibly.

## 3. Glassblower Rhythm Cut

**Use case:** native-audio craft montage · **Format:** 15s, 16:9 · **Best mode:** artisan + workshop references, custom multi-shot

**Why it works:** The edit rhythm follows real craft sounds, while heat, tool contact and breath remain tied to visible actions.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 16:9, documentary craft montage driven entirely by synchronized workshop sound, four shots.

[LOCKS]
Adult glassblower Eli wears clear face shield, dark cotton shirt, heat-resistant sleeves and gloves. One orange molten glass gather on one steel blowpipe. Brick reheating furnace frame left, metal bench center. Preserve PPE, tool and glass color progression.

[TIMED SHOTS AND SOUND CUES]
0–4s — Close side shot. Eli rotates the blowpipe steadily on bench rails; one metal-on-metal rolling rhythm at constant tempo. Molten glass stays centered by gravity and rotation.
4–8s — Macro insert. Wet wooden block cups the rotating gather with controlled steam: one soft hiss beginning only after contact. No bare hand approaches heat.
8–11s — Medium. Eli places pipe end to lips and gives one short controlled breath; glass expands slightly into a round bubble while rotation continues. Breath is audible, not exaggerated.
11–15s — Close on steel shears making one shallow trim in hot glass, producing a crisp snip. Hard cut on the snip to the cooled finished vessel standing on a plain shelf; one fingertip glass ring lands after the cut. Hold in quiet.

[AUDIO]
No added music. Rail rhythm provides tempo; steam hiss, breath, furnace rumble, shears and final glass ring are isolated, natural and exactly synchronized.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Approved demonstration by trained adult with PPE. One blowpipe and one glass object. No unsafe touch, facial proximity to furnace, exploding glass, duplicated tools, impossible instant cooling shown within a shot, generated narration, text, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** glassblowing → pottery / bookbinding / metal engraving; build the edit around four genuine, reviewable sound events.

**Failure check:** Mute and unmute playback: every featured sound must have one visible cause and occur at contact, not before it.

## 4. Quiet Docking Above the Aurora

**Use case:** orbital physics visualization · **Format:** 15s, 21:9 · **Best mode:** vehicle references + custom multi-shot

**Why it works:** Low relative velocity, fixed axes and sparse thruster pulses create readable microgravity instead of chaotic destruction.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 21:9, realistic near-future orbital docking visualization, calm procedural tone, three shots.

[VEHICLE LOCKS]
Service craft: small white cylinder, four folded rectangular solar panels, black circular docking ring at nose, no crew visible. Station: gray research module with matching axial port and two long blue solar arrays. Blank surfaces, no national or company marks.

[ORIENTATION]
Station docking axis runs screen left-to-right. Station port faces frame left; service craft approaches from frame left nose-first. Earth limb remains low background with a faint auroral band. Sunlight direction stays upper right.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–5s — Long-lens side view. Craft closes distance slowly at less than one body length over five seconds. Two tiny lateral thruster pulses correct upward drift; exhaust is brief and subtle. Station remains inertially steady.
5–10s — Camera cuts to station-port view looking outward. Craft nose stays centered using one short roll correction under five degrees, then stops all visible rotation. No rapid zoom.
10–15s — Exterior three-quarter. Docking rings meet at near-zero relative speed, compress slightly and lock with one small mechanical movement. Solar arrays do not flex. End with both vehicles moving as one while Earth drifts slowly behind.

[AUDIO]
Exterior shots have no diegetic sound. A restrained non-diegetic tonal bed continues. At contact, cut briefly to an interior structural microphone perspective for one muted latch thump and two confirmation clicks; no voice-over.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Visualization, not operational instruction. Preserve ports, axes, solar panels, light and low relative speed. No explosion, debris, visible exterior sound wave, large flame, astronaut, flag, readable UI, mission claim, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** orbital docking → underwater ROV capture / warehouse drone landing / robotic arm handoff; define axes and relative velocity.

**Failure check:** Check approach direction, roll amount, contact speed, array rigidity and the deliberate interior-only contact sound.
