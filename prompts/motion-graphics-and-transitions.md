# Motion Graphics & Transition Prompts

[← Catalog](README.md) · [Prompting guide](../docs/PROMPT-GUIDE.md)

Four original Kling recipes for script-led product graphics, multi-photo continuity, material-driven transitions and extreme scale reveals. Supply approved reference images and add exact typography in post-production.

## 1. Refill Tin: Script-to-Motion-Graphics Ad

**Use case:** script + product reference motion-graphics ad · **Format:** 15s, 1:1 · **Best mode:** script board + product image + start/end frames

**Why it works:** The script reference controls beat order only; product geometry, graphic shapes and voice-over each receive separate roles.

```text
[OUTPUT]
15 seconds, 1:1, bright tactile mixed-media product film, four beats, clean negative space for post-production copy.

[REFERENCE ROLES]
Image 1 is a three-beat script board and controls sequence only; ignore its fonts, colors and people. Image 2 locks one unbranded cylindrical tea-refill tin: brushed silver body, flat teal lid, shallow vertical seam. Start frame locks centered closed tin; end frame locks the same tin beside one paper refill pouch.

[GRAPHIC SYSTEM]
Only circles, straight paper strips and soft teal shadows. Off-white background, teal and apricot accents. No generated words, numbers or icons.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–4s — Tin centered. Two apricot paper circles roll in from opposite sides and stop behind it like a simple sunrise. Camera locked.
4–8s — Lid lifts vertically 3cm; one kraft-paper refill pouch slides from frame left and pours a controlled ribbon of loose tea into the tin. Tea level stops below the rim; pouch does not deform.
8–12s — Overhead match cut. Teal paper strips fold into a circular loop around tin and pouch, without becoming a logo. Camera rotates 20 degrees clockwise while objects remain fixed.
12–15s — Return to three-quarter hero frame matching the supplied end image. Lid closes once; pouch stands beside tin. Hold clean upper-right space for verified copy.

[AUDIO]
Calm English voice-over: “Keep the tin. Refill what you use.” Paper roll, tea pour and soft lid click synchronized to actions. Two-note marimba motif, no vocals.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Preserve tin and pouch geometry; one of each. The script reference never controls branding or appearance. No product claim beyond exact voice-over, generated text, recycling certification, leaf logo, duplicate item or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** tea tin → soap dispenser / spice jar / pencil case; replace environmental implications with reviewed, product-specific wording.

**Failure check:** Compare first and last tin silhouettes against references and confirm all exact copy remains reserved for post.

## 2. From Seedling to Supper

**Use case:** fluid multi-photo process transition · **Format:** 12s, 16:9 · **Best mode:** four owned image references

**Why it works:** Each image has a declared subject and correspondence point, so transitions follow shape, direction and season rather than melting everything together.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 16:9, four-stage farm-to-table visual poem, smooth realistic transitions, no people or branding.

[REFERENCE ROLES]
Image 1 locks a tomato seedling in a terracotta pot. Image 2 locks one mature tomato cluster on vine. Image 3 locks one red tomato on a wooden cutting board. Image 4 locks one bowl of tomato soup on a linen table. Use only the central subject and its surface; ignore unrelated background objects.

[CORRESPONDENCE]
Keep the main round red form centered at 55% frame width. Growth direction is bottom-to-top; the final camera direction remains left-to-right. Use leaf, circular fruit and steam shapes as visual bridges.

[TIMED TRANSITIONS]
0–3s — Macro push toward seedling. Two leaves unfold naturally; the central leaf fills frame.
3–6s — Cut within leaf occlusion to mature vine. Camera continues the same push. One green tomato changes to red through a short time-lapse while its position and size stay fixed.
6–9s — The red tomato rotates only 20 degrees; a matching red circular wipe formed by its surface reveals the same tomato resting on the cutting board. A knife remains parked safely outside frame edge and never moves.
9–12s — Camera passes behind a rising soft steam veil from off-screen cooking; reveal finished soup bowl with one basil leaf. End on a stable overhead composition.

[AUDIO]
One continuous gentle room tone evolves from garden birds to kitchen ambience. Leaf brush, soft wooden contact and quiet simmer bridge transitions. No narration or music.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Four stages in fixed order. No liquid morph directly from raw whole tomato, impossible knife action, hands, duplicated fruit, reversing camera, text, logo or watermark. Transition occlusions must hide, not display, geometry changes.
```

**Swap ideas:** clay → cup / fiber → fabric / sketch → built object; select one measurable correspondence shape per transition.

**Failure check:** Scrub each seam frame by frame; direction, center point and surface color should continue without a visible melt.

## 3. Blue Glaze, Tidal Map

**Use case:** material-match transition sequence · **Format:** 10s, 21:9 · **Best mode:** craft object + coastline reference images

**Why it works:** Similar blue linework creates transitions, while explicit occlusions prevent a ceramic object from arbitrarily liquefying.

```text
[OUTPUT]
10 seconds, 21:9, elegant craft-to-landscape transition film, three shots, rich but natural blue palette.

[LOCKS]
One shallow white ceramic plate with an original cobalt branching glaze pattern. One aerial reference of a fictional rocky coastline with branching blue inlets. No identifiable map or cultural motif.

[TIMED SHOTS]
0–3s — 100mm macro glides across the dry plate surface from lower left to upper right. Cobalt line fills more of frame; glaze remains solid and reflective.
3–6s — Camera reaches a dark cobalt pool until blue occupies the full frame. Cut on full-color occlusion to an aerial ocean surface whose inlet line continues at the same angle and width. Camera keeps moving lower-left to upper-right.
6–8s — A thin cloud bank crosses and fully covers frame. Under cloud occlusion, transition to a clean architectural paper model whose blue channels repeat the coastline branching pattern.
8–10s — Camera stops above the full paper model; one warm raking light reveals relief. Hold clean lower-right space for a real title added in post.

[AUDIO]
Soft ceramic fingertip ring at 1s, distant tide after first transition, paper rustle after second. One continuous low bowed-glass tone connects all shots.

[CONSTRAINTS]
Transitions occur only under full blue or cloud occlusion. Plate never melts; ocean never spills into model; pattern and camera direction remain coherent. No real map label, generated text, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** wood grain → river delta / fabric pleat → dunes / metal brushing → rain; use owned textures and avoid protected ornamental designs.

**Failure check:** Overlay seam frames and confirm pattern angle, line width, camera velocity and audio bridge align.

## 4. The Crystal Canyon Is Breakfast

**Use case:** extreme macro-to-table scale reveal · **Format:** 12s, 16:9 · **Best mode:** start/end reference frames

**Why it works:** The reveal is achieved by continuous camera scale and fixed landmarks, not by transforming the miniature world into food.

```text
[OUTPUT]
12 seconds, 16:9, playful photorealistic scale reveal, one continuous apparent pullback, sunrise palette.

[START AND END]
Start reference: amber translucent crystal canyon with one dark ridge left and a pale granular plain center. End reference: macro sugar crust on one caramelized pastry on a blue ceramic plate, same ridge and plain positions. Preserve landmark correspondence.

[CAMERA PATH]
0–4s — Camera glides backward through the amber canyon at slow walking speed. Warm light comes from upper right; tiny granular particles stay fixed to ground.
4–8s — Pullback accelerates gently. Depth of field expands; canyon walls reveal repeating sugar-crystal facets. A distant low rumble resolves into room ventilation. No cut or morph is visible.
8–12s — Camera crosses from extreme macro to table-scale view, revealing the entire canyon as the pastry's crisp sugar surface. Continue backward until blue plate, linen napkin and morning window light are visible. One adult hand sets a plain coffee cup 15cm away without touching pastry. Hold two seconds.

[AUDIO]
Low canyon-like air gradually resolves into quiet kitchen room tone. Very small sugar crackle, cup contact at 10s and no music or narration.

[CONSTRAINTS]
One pastry, plate, cup and hand. Landmarks keep position during scale change. The canyon is always the real surface at macro scale; no miniature people, buildings, falling rocks, melting, bite, text, logo or watermark.
```

**Swap ideas:** frost landscape → cold bottle / sand dunes → handmade paper / metallic city → circuit board; plan identical landmarks in both reference frames.

**Failure check:** Reject any result with a hidden cut, landmark jump, food deformation or scale change before the camera earns it.
